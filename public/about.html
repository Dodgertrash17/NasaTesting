About this project

SkyBlink was built for a NASA hackathon challenge: make a public web tool that shows how the sky changes over time in images from the SPHEREx mission, so anyone can help spot moving objects.

Data & credits

SkyBlink only shows public data. Each source below has its own terms of use, and we follow them. The search page lets you choose which of these each search uses: SPHEREx images are always loaded, Gaia and WISE positions only when ticked.

SPHEREx images
SPHEREx Level 2 spectral images, from the NASA/IPAC Infrared Science Archive (IRSA) and its public copy on AWS. SPHEREx is a NASA mission managed by the Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL) and the California Institute of Technology (Caltech). Image credit: NASA/JPL-Caltech. Our data access follows the IRSA SPHEREx tutorial notebooks.
IRSA
This project has made use of the NASA/IPAC Infrared Science Archive, which is funded by the National Aeronautics and Space Administration and operated by the California Institute of Technology.
WISE positions (CatWISE2020)
The CatWISE2020 catalogue (Marocco et al. 2021, ApJS 253, 8), built from WISE and NEOWISE data, via IRSA. WISE and NEOWISE are NASA missions (JPL/Caltech, UCLA).
Gaia DR3 positions
This work has made use of data from the European Space Agency (ESA) mission Gaia, processed by the Gaia Data Processing and Analysis Consortium (DPAC). Funding for the DPAC has been provided by national institutions, in particular the institutions participating in the Gaia Multilateral Agreement. We use Gaia Data Release 3 (Gaia Collaboration, Vallenari et al. 2023, A&A 674, A1), read through IRSA's copy of the catalogue.
Known asteroids
For the pre-processed targets, one-night objects are checked against known asteroids and comets with SkyBoT (IMCCE, Observatoire de Paris; Berthier et al. 2006).
WiseView
The "Open in WiseView" button links to WiseView (Caselden et al. 2018), the WISE blink tool used by the Backyard Worlds project. It is a separate site; SkyBlink does not send it any data except the sky position in the link.

Images labelled "Simulated" are made-up practice data, not real observations.
